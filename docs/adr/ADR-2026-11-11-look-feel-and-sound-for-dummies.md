# Look, feel and sound, explained simply

- **Technical version:** [ADR-2026-11-11-look-feel-and-sound.md](ADR-2026-11-11-look-feel-and-sound.md)

## What is being decided

How Bluff looks, moves and sounds. The goal: when someone in Lagos, Kano or Enugu opens it, it should feel like sitting down at a game they already know, like Ludo on a wooden board, not like a computer program.

## What you'll see

**A real table.** A dark wooden board, seen at a slight angle, with the four colours every Ludo player knows: red, green, yellow and blue. Each player sits on their own side with their picture, their name and a calabash cup holding their dice. When you lose a die, it leaves your cup, so everyone can see who is winning.

**Dice that really roll.** Your cup shakes, the dice fly out, bounce off the edges and tumble to a stop, quickly, and differently every time. They always land on your real hidden numbers. When someone calls a bluff, every cup lifts and all the dice roll out for everyone to see.

**Cowries for chips.** Your chips are shown as cowrie shells, the old West African money. The pot in the middle of the board is a little pile of cowries.

**Buttons you want to press.** Buttons look like raised tiles and press down when you tap them. Cards lift and tilt. Messages arrive as little tiles that drop into place, then leave on their own.

**Game icons, not app icons.** The profile, settings, sound and chip icons come from game and 3D icon packs we're licensed to use, not the plain line icons every app uses.

**Day and night.** Warm sand and wood in the day; deep indigo with lantern light at night.

## Easy to find your way

- **The board is always in the middle of the screen,** on a phone or a laptop.
- **Everything you can do right now sits in one place, just under the board,** where your thumb reaches. A single sentence above it tells you what to do next, like "Your turn: raise the bid or call bluff".
- **Bidding is one simple grid.** It shows every bid you're allowed to make, numbers down the side and dice faces across the top, and the ones you can't make are greyed out. There are also three one-tap suggestions. The button always says your bid in words, and the game shows how many of your own dice count towards it.
- **Pop-up windows look like carved wooden panels.** On phones they slide up from the bottom.
- **A profile page** where you pick your face from 12 illustrated ones (gele, fila, cap, beads, braids), change your name, see your chips and your wins, and set music and sounds. Your face is saved on the game itself, so everyone at the table sees it.

## What you'll hear

**Music,** built on the talking drum, shekere, udu and kalimba: calm in the lobby, livelier at the table.

**Sounds** for the cup shaking, the dice rolling on wood, a knock when someone bids, a drum hit for "Call bluff", cowries rattling when chips arrive, and a short celebration when you win.

Music and sounds each have their own on and off switch, and the game remembers your choice. Nothing ever plays before you tap something. Every sound has a matching message on screen, so you never miss anything with the sound off.

## Fair to every phone

Many players will be on everyday Android phones and paying for data. So:

- The front page stays light, with no heavy 3D.
- The 3D table only downloads when you sit down at a table, and your phone keeps it for next time.
- If a phone struggles, or you prefer less motion, the game switches to a simpler, flat board that plays exactly the same.

## Where the art and sound come from

Nearly everything comes from free, proven collections: fonts by Nigerian designers, free game sounds, free wood textures, a free 3D icon set and a free set of hand-drawn faces. It all costs nothing. The few things nobody offers for free (adire-style patterns, gele and fila headwear) are drawn for Bluff. Everything is credited. Nothing is copied from another game. The project owner hears and approves every sound before it goes in.

## What it costs

About an extra week of work, mostly design. Before any game screen is built, the owner sees and approves a clickable sample: the board, a dice roll, a button, a message tile and the front page.

## Later

The words stay short and simple. After the competition, a Pidgin version would be a natural next step.
